Late is better than never, so here I am writing about my second Bingham rugby tournament ever! That was back in August though. I had also planned trip to Thailand around it to show some friends my home country, and also to spend more time with my family after the tournament as well. I just got back to my desk not long ago, and I guess it look that long for me to settle down and find time to put proverbial pen to paper.

A little context, I spent my university years, and almost the decade after pretty much hunker down in my home city. Between studying, getting started on my career, and also trying to speed run through PR and citizenship process, I didn't travel much. Even going home to Thailand was pretty minimal. Then 2020 happened, and the traveling stopped for a few years. Then in 2024, at the end of my first season with [Vancouver Rogues](https://blog.rogues-rugby.ca/), we had committed to send a team out to Bingham 2024, which I of course registered to go. It wasn't until I started putting together my itinerary that it dawned on me this was going to be my first adult trip internationally with my friends. I got really excited with anticipation and imagination. We ended up doing Berlin as a pre-tournament vacation, then head off to Rome for my very first Bingham tournament.

From that one trip, I knew rugby trips will become my big excuse to travel, and a new line item in my budget cheat. In December of that same year, I made it into the US for the first time, to go to Rucktacular in San Francisco, another more local rugby tournament. Even smaller trip like a local trip to Nanaimo for a boot camp, or Kelowna for a weekend game, traveling with the Rogues always are part of the highlights of my year.

Anyway, the trip. The second time is even better than the first! I got a chance to show a special someone I had met at the last tournament Thailand pre-tournament, and made some really good memories together. There were lots of recognizable faces from previous tournament whether from socials or from the pitch. There were some new friends I said hello to for the first time as well. I guess I'm more international than ever now.

I don't want to go too deep into the vacation side of the trip. Maybe in another post. Today, we're here to talk about rugby. Well, not too much rugby; you can learn about rugby from anywhere. I highly encourage checking out [IGR](https://igrugby.org/) and its [Bingham Cup](https://www.binghamcup.com/) tournament. They do good work creating inclusive rugby environment for many. What I want to talk about is the personal experience I had at the Bingham rugby tournament.

Let's start with the beautiful opening ceremony. Being in a room full of queer and inclusive team sports was especially powerful for someone who didn't feel comfortable with the whole sports and locker room culture of the late 90's and early 2000's. Walking into the room, and looking around, recognizing we are working together to create this space for each other made you feel belong. The ceremony started with greetings of all the organizers, then the guests were invited to share a few words. There were a lot of politicians from city to national level sharing their messages in support of the event. The message was a little generic, PR-crafted, and was a little too focus on the fact that the 2032 Olympic was going to be in Brisbane. I was a little checked out. But then Sharni Smale, an out lesbian professional Australian rugby player, and an Olympian, was also there, and gave a beautiful speech. Amanda herself was also at the ceremony again, and gave a nice welcoming speech to also kick-off the Amanda cup, the W+ side of the tournament. It was very lovely. As aside, I also got to speak with Amanda personally the next morning when we got to our first field as well. It was a very cute moment for us. The ceremony ended with a little social time for teams to meet each other. We also have a tradition of mascot-napping, so people were scoping out the other teams' mascot carrier.

The second tournament is truly many folds better than the first. There was just so much more anticipation of the games, the trip, the socials, knowing what's coming up. And also with even more people you're looking forward to meeting. Some specific teams you want to play against because your friends are their players, teams you have a rivalry with from the pervious tournament, teams you want to redeem yourself against, or a new team from a different place with a different play style you want to test yourself against. Hearing about the teams that will be in our bracket, and learning about them in anticipation hyped up the experience the way scrolling through travel vlogs before your trip gives you. The more you talk about them, the longer time seems to last before the game starts.

We went into the tournament with abundant confidence. We started our Bingham 2024 playing in division 3, and got bumped up to division 2 by the end. Our local rugby league in BC is very competitive, and we play so many games. So so many games each season compare to many other teams at the Bingham tournament. This year, we started Bingham in division 2 based on our previous performance; but we, as a team, have also so much more experience now, and we're definitely here to play rugby first, social fun after some good rugby games.

The team performed so well, and I am so proud of us. Once again we got bumped up to division 1 😅 by the end. We made it to the final within the division 1 along side another team that also got bumped up with us, though we ended up losing by a single point. But no matter, we still have so much more room to grow. We're not at our peak yet. We will be starting next Bingham tournament in division 1, and we will be coming back better than ever. We've been practicing hard since we came back despite so little rest we got all year between last season of BC rugby, Bingham training, and now we're in another season of BC rugby. Our teammates are still showing up, and we're still going hard at it, and getting better every week.

But regardless of how competitive the games get, stepping on a field to play fun clean rugby games against your friends is very exciting. It gives a similar feeling of a very serious MtG night, a very Seto Kaiba vs Yugi Moto. Not something I ever got to experience competing in CrossFit. CrossFit is a very individual sport with performance comparison. What I do don't directly impact other competitors on the floor, or do I react to what the other people are doing. Rugby, though, is about reacting and taking an initiative to force a reaction from the other players. It's a very interactive kind of competition that I was not familiar with before, but find so exhilarating. Standing there staring down the opposite players of you on the field. Watching, and analyzing. The anticipation to act, and the aggression simmering to unleash. It's a very exciting game to play.

After the game, and the adrenaline rush is gone. I get analytical about what happened, why it happened; what they did, or didn't do; what I was expected of them, or how things went in an unexpected way. And in someway, I got to know the other players on the fields, my side, or the other, a little more. And when I get to meet them at the social, and learn about who they are outside of the game, it's just as exciting to find them unexpectedly different, or how their personality carry over into their play style. And the connection we make feels deep.

Then there's the social aspect, and there was a lot of social aspects. You could probably imagine what putting thousands of gay men, emphasis on men, in the same proximity would do. I won't lie there were some eye catchers I was excited to meet and get acquainted with. Not that I actually did much of that since there was a special person I wanted to spend all my time with. Despite the busy playing schedule, and our obligation to our own respective team, I try to spend as much time as I can with him, while trying to be conscious of his space and his obligations. No matter how little, I was glad to get to spend time with him, and get to meet his teammates. I had butterfly in my stomach on cloud nine the most of my time outside the field.

But like I said, my team was certainly there to play rugby first, so our fun time in between matches were pretty minimal, and low key, since we want to always be well-rested, and ready for the next match. I missed out on a few big events, but I was happy to make that trade-off. It wasn't until the last night of the tournament that we finally breathed the sign of relief that the rugby obligation was over, and the unrestrained fun could begin.

There was an official party after the closing ceremony. Lots of people enjoy the music, dancing, and what the bar has to offer. Some prefer something more adulterated, and headed off to some other location appropriate for the activities. I stuck around to enjoy the company of my special person for as long as I could. But after 2 hard fought games with such an early start time, my body gave out pretty quick, and I had to head back to the hotel too soon.

Sadly being in close proximity with that many people, it's bound to happen. I got lucky last time, but not so this time. I caught a pretty major flu, and was out of commission for several days. My special person left for home while I was recuperating in my hotel room, so I didn't get a chance to see him off. I was very sad for many days. And still feeling sad about it now.

For the last leg of the trip, I headed off to Sydney for my own travel time for several days, before heading back to Thailand for a little recuperation, family visit, and figuring out my next step. I ended up staying in Thailand for about another month before flying back to Canada. Just in time to start the 2026 rugby season with the team.

I'm closing this with the emphasis that rugby trips and traveling the Rogues are super fun, and super important to me now, but I've also traveled to a festival on my own since, and would love to also do more of that too. You could say I've been bitten a bit by a travel bug. It certainly isn't cheap but I'm happy to make a place for it in my life, and make it part of my priorities. When there's a will, I will find a way.
